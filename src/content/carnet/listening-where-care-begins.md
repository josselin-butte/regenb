As a conservatoire-trained musician and sound engineer, I learnt one simple thing early on: before you can play in tune, you have to listen. I believe the same is true of the people, teams and places I accompany.

:::essentiel
- **Listening is already the beginning of care.** For Joan Tronto, care begins with attentiveness: noticing what someone else needs [1, 2].
- **Observe before you intervene.** It is the first principle of permaculture: observe, then interact, with the smallest useful intervention [3, 4].
- **Listening involves the body.** The sounds of the living world are linked to less stress and better mood [5].
- **For anyone who accompanies others, the lesson is simple:** begin every gathering with a moment of listening, to the place, to others and to yourself.
:::

## Care begins with attentiveness

The philosopher Joan Tronto places attentiveness at the very start of care: being able to set our own plans aside for a moment to notice what someone else needs [1]. She then describes further phases, up to caring with others, in solidarity [2].

With Berenice Fisher, she defines care as everything we do to maintain, continue and repair our world: our bodies, ourselves, our environment [6]. To me, that is a beautiful definition of regeneration.

## Observe, then interact

Permaculture says it in its own way. Its first principle is “observe and interact” [3]. Look for a long time, act a little, then observe again: the smallest useful intervention, and then you watch what it does [4]. Listening is that observation, turned towards others.

## Coming back to your senses

Listening also involves the body. When we shift from thinking about to perceiving, we slow down. A meta-analysis published in PNAS links natural sounds, water and birdsong, to less stress and better mood [5]. Results vary from one study to another, and I don’t promise miracles: I offer time to come back to your senses.

## A thread through the five capacities

At RegenB France, listening links the five capacities I work with:

- **Earth, clarifying your field:** listening to the place and context before acting.
- **Water, welcoming and including:** hearing every voice, even the quietest.
- **Fire, using power with discernment:** listening before deciding.
- **Air, holding paradoxes:** hearing several voices at once.
- **Space, moving forward with love and attention:** listening again, over time, to how action was received.

:::accompagnement
**Something to try: the three distances (5 minutes).** Settle in, eyes closed or gaze resting on the floor. First listen to the furthest sounds, outside. Then to the sounds in the room. Then to the closest ones: your breath, your body. One minute for each distance. There is nothing to get right: if a thought comes, let it pass and come back to the sounds.
:::

We need more care in everything we do. It often begins with an attentive ear.
